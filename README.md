## Summary of findings

### Mini-Project 1 — District Population Forecaster

* **Fastest relative growth: Wakiso** (CAGR 6.5 %/yr). Kampala and Gulu share an identical CAGR (4.6 %) because both grew by a factor of 1.5, which illustrates that CAGR ignores the path taken.
* **Model selection by hold-out (train 2015–21, test 2022–24):** exponential/CAGR growth won for Kampala, Wakiso, Gulu and Arua (test MAPE 0.4–4.1 %), and a linear trend won for Mbarara (MAPE 0.2 %). The **Fibonacci-ratio** model failed everywhere (MAPE 78–92 %) because it forces growth of about 62 % a year and learns nothing from the data.
* **Classrooms needed by 2029** (18 % school-age, 53 pupils per room): about **4,960 in total** (95 % range 4,280–5,640). The largest single need is in Wakiso (≈ 2,090), not Kampala (≈ 1,540), because growth rather than size drives new demand.
* A smooth forecast has low variance, but it is not low-*uncertainty*. The bootstrap intervals (1,000 residual resamples) show that Arua's forecast, distorted by an influx shock, carries an interval of about 18 % of the forecast value, against under 1 % for Mbarara.

### Mini-Project 2 — Solar Micro-Grid Dispatch Planner

* The system 3x + 2y = D1, 4x + y = D2 is **well-posed**: det = −5 and condition number ≈ 5.8, so demand errors are amplified at most about 5.8× (less than one significant digit lost).
* Cramer's rule gives x = (2D2 − D1)/5 and y = (4D1 − 3D2)/5, so a non-negative dispatch exists only when **0.75 ≤ D1/D2 ≤ 2**. In the synthetic month, **5 of 30 days** failed this test — all four Sundays and one Saturday — because clinic load drops while critical load stays flat.
* Repairing those days is a policy choice. NNLS was cheapest but left ≈ 19 kWh of **critical** load unmet. I used a least-cost linear programme (`cover`) that never under-supplies critical equipment.
* The **battery is the volatile source**: coefficient of variation ≈ 55 % against ≈ 10 % for solar. At three times the unit price it drives the ≈ **UGX 270,000** monthly cost and its weekly rhythm (weekdays ≈ UGX 10,500/day, Sundays ≈ 3,800).
* Solving all 30 days in one vectorised `scipy.linalg.solve` call was ≈ 10× faster than a loop, rising to several hundred times faster at 3,000 days.
* Extensions: a `HybridMicroGrid` with diesel and a night-load constraint removes every infeasible day, but at a much higher cost. A dependent third row makes the system singular, with either infinitely many solutions or none (a rank test). A ±5 % Monte Carlo shows battery draw swinging by about ±45 %, with amplification reaching the condition number bound (5.81 vs 5.83).

### Mini-Project 3 — Lake Victoria Fish Stock & Export Risk

* Fibonacci "stock" grows by a fixed ~62 % per step forever. It has no carrying capacity, mortality or harvest, so it cannot say what catch is sustainable.
* With logistic growth and harvesting (r = 0.4/week, K = 10,000 t), the long-run yield hK(1 − h/r) peaks at **h = r/2 = 0.20**, giving **MSY = rK/4 = 1,000 t/week**. The h = 0.20 scenario lands 98 % of MSY in its first year and holds the stock at K/2. h = 0.10 is safe but forgoes about 25 % of the attainable catch. **h = 0.30 catches less than h = 0.20 while halving the stock** (growth overfishing).
* A **variance threshold is meaningless**, because variance is in UGX². The same revenue passes or fails the "50,000" rule depending on whether it is recorded in shillings or billions. CV is scale-free.
* Across 1,000 bounded random-walk price paths, annual-revenue CV ≈ 10–11 % (Moderate) for all sustainable rates. The 5 % VaR is ≈ 16 % of expected revenue (UGX 72 bn at h = 0.10).
* The collapsing policy (h = 0.45) has the *lowest* revenue CV. `RiskAssessor` therefore combines financial risk with an ecological floor based on stock status.
* An 8-week closed season **lowers** 5-year revenue by 9–15 % at or below the MSY rate, but **raises** it by 7 % at h = 0.30 (in every price path). It is a correction for over-fishing, not a free gain.

### Mini-Project 4 — Rainfall Pattern & Crop Suitability

* Crop thresholds are derived from cited sources: **maize 85–190 mm/month and beans 80–160 mm/month** (FAO 1986 seasonal water needs ÷ growing period), and **Robusta coffee 55–200 mm/month** (UCDA 2019 *Robusta Coffee Handbook*). Every region-month is classified as good, drought risk or waterlogging risk.
* Last year's `math.cos()` method is wrong: it gives a region a similarity of 0.43 *with itself*. The corrected cosine (dot ÷ norms) matches `scipy.spatial.distance.cosine` exactly.
* Cosine rates every pair as "similar" (≥ 0.75) because rainfall vectors are all-positive and cosine ignores magnitude: a region three times wetter scores 1.0. **Pearson** shows that Kampala and Gulu have unrelated seasonal timing (r ≈ −0.07).
* Circular `find_peaks` with a 30 % prominence threshold finds Mbarara **bimodal** and Gulu **unimodal**, both correct. It finds the illustrative Kampala series unimodal, because that series barely has short rains. On **10 years of real NASA POWER data** it recovers all three known zones, including Kampala as bimodal.
* Real data show large year-to-year variability (rainy-month CV ≈ 20–60 %). The advisory for Mbarara therefore rests on reliability: bean seasons in March–May and September–November had enough rain in 9–10 years out of 10.

### Mini-Project 5 — Taxi Route Revenue, Pricing & Fleet

* Entebbe earns **57 % of revenue** from about 40 % of the passengers, because of its UGX 5,000 fare.
* Ntinda fare equilibrium (solved with `scipy.linalg.solve`): **P\* = UGX 2,200, Q\* = 76** passengers per trip-hour. The current UGX 2,000 is **below equilibrium**, leaving a shortage of 10 passengers per trip-hour. Demand is inelastic there (−0.5), so clearing the shortage by raising the fare would lift revenue per trip-hour by ≈ 19 %; adding vehicles is the alternative.
* The walk-forward backtest (days 4–10) shows that the **naive forecast beats every model**. The α grid search picks α = 1, which *is* naive. The original 3-day moving average is 30–60 % worse, and the linear trend is worst. The series behave like random walks, so smoothing only adds lag.
* The day-11 revenue forecast is **≈ UGX 560,000** (Ntinda 90k, Entebbe 320k, Mukono 150k). **One vehicle per route** covers the forecast plus a 15 % buffer (40–57 % load factor). If demand is one-directional, Entebbe and Mukono need a standby second vehicle.
* Extension: on 60 simulated days with a Friday peak and a Sunday trough, **seasonal naive cuts the MAE of the moving average by a factor of 1.8–2.9** (Wilcoxon p ≤ 0.014 on all routes).

## Data sources

All data are **illustrative** values taken from the assignment brief, or synthetic.

* Project 1: the two extra districts (Mbarara, Arua) are my own illustrative series, not UBOS figures.
* Project 2: `data/kasese_demand.csv` is generated by the notebook with a seeded random generator (weekly clinic pattern plus Gaussian noise). Unit costs, including diesel at UGX 1,200/kWh, are illustrative.
* Project 3: all biological and price parameters (r, K, N0, price band, volatility) are illustrative values from the brief or my own assumptions; prices are simulated.
* Project 4 (real data, extension): NASA Langley Research Center POWER Project, monthly `PRECTOTCORR_SUM` (API v2.10, MERRA-2-based), 2014–2023, for Kampala (0.348° N, 32.583° E), Gulu (2.772° N, 32.288° E) and Mbarara (0.607° S, 30.654° E). https://power.larc.nasa.gov — saved in `data/nasa_power_monthly_rainfall.csv`.
* Project 5: Ntinda passenger counts are from the original question; the Entebbe and Mukono counts, demand/supply curves and the 60-day series are illustrative or simulated (seeded).
* Project 4 crop thresholds: FAO (1986) *Irrigation Water Management: Irrigation Water Needs*, Training Manual No. 3, ch. 2, Tables 4–5 (https://www.fao.org/4/s2022e/s2022e02.htm); Uganda Coffee Development Authority (2019) *Robusta Coffee Handbook*.
